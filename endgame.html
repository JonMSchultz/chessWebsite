FINISH STRONG

Endgame strategies.

When fewer pieces remain, every tempo matters. Learn to activate your king, create passed pawns, and convert basic winning positions.

BEGINNER

Basic endgames

King and pawn versus king

Your king is an active piece in the endgame. Support your pawn, use your king to gain space, and calculate whether the pawn can promote safely.

Opposition

When kings face each other with one square between them, the player who is not to move may have the opposition. This can force the opposing king to give way and allow progress.

The square of the pawn

Imagine a square from the pawn's file to the promotion square, with the same number of ranks as the pawn has remaining. If the defending king can enter that square when it is the king's turn to move, it may catch the pawn without help.

King activity and promotion

Centralize your king when it is safe. A passed pawn has no opposing pawn able to stop it on its file or an adjacent file, so it can become a powerful promotion threat.

INTERMEDIATE

Rook endgames

Active rook placement

Rooks are often strongest when active. Place them on open files, attack targets, and seek activity rather than passively defending pawns.

Rook behind passed pawns

A rook is often well placed behind a passed pawn—whether it is your pawn or the opponent's—because its influence increases as the pawn advances.

Basic rook endgame principles

Keep the rook active, use the king, create targets, and be alert to checking distance. A passive rook can make even an extra pawn difficult to convert.

Checkmating patterns

King and queen / king and rook versus king

Use your major piece to restrict the enemy king, bring your own king closer, and steadily drive the defender to the edge. Avoid stalemate and coordinate the pieces rather than giving random checks.

Two-rook checkmate

Two rooks can create a ladder mate by controlling ranks or files in turn, pushing the enemy king toward the edge of the board.

Advanced concepts

Zugzwang and triangulation

Zugzwang is a position where having to move is a disadvantage. Triangulation is a king maneuver that loses a tempo and can place the opponent in zugzwang.

Outside passed pawns

An outside passed pawn is far from the other pawns. It can draw the enemy king away, allowing your king to collect pawns or support a second passed pawn.
